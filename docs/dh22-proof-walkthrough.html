Dark Horse 22: the symmetry group, with the proofs

Status: draft for critique. Not peer reviewed. Not published. The cards are machine-rendered from the paper’s statements. The proofs are by hand.

Account names appear only in the Cast table of the paper. This page uses the paper’s labels p1–p28.

1. The result

Take the 22 Dark Horse co-posts as a structure: 28 accounts (points) and 22 posts (blocks). A symmetry is a shuffling of the accounts that maps the posts onto the posts. The symmetries form a group, and its order is the number of symmetries:

Aut(S₂₂) = H ≅ S₉ × (S₂ ≀ S₃) × S₄ × S₂ × S₂, of order 2¹⁶·3⁶·5·7 = 1,672,151,040.

The 28 accounts fall into 10 orbits: five groups that can be shuffled among themselves (sizes 9, 6, 4, 2, 2), and five accounts that never move (p₁, p₄, p₆, p₉, p₂₁).

2. Reading the symbols

Sₙ is the group of all ways to shuffle n things. S₄ has 24 shuffles.

A ≀ B (wreath product) is a block-by-block shuffle. In S₂ ≀ S₃: there are three pairs of accounts. You may swap the two accounts inside any pair, and you may permute the three pairs. Its order is 2³·3! = 48.

× means independent shuffles on separate sets of accounts. The factors of H act on disjoint sets, so they commute.

Orbit: the accounts an account can be moved to by some symmetry.

Order (|G|): the total number of symmetries.

3. Comparison with the first paper

The first paper has a 7-post structure (S₇, order 12, the group D₆) and a 492-post structure (S₄₉₂, order about 3.3×10³⁰). The Dark Horse structure (S₂₂) sits between them. All three share the definitions, the twin lemma (Lemma 2.2) and the pendant-star lemma (Lemma 3.1).

4. The lower bound: H is a symmetry group

Lemma 3.1 (pendant stars): if several disjoint sets of accounts all sit on the same core of posts, their internal shuffles are symmetries. This gives the factors of H. Each factor acts only on its own leaf set and leaves everything else fixed.

5. The upper bound: nothing else is a symmetry

This is the hard direction, proved by hand. It uses replication numbers (how many posts an account is on) and traces (which fixed points a post contains).

  • (a) Fixed points. The replication numbers 22, 5, 3, 2 each occur exactly once, at p₁, p₆, p₄, p₉. So every symmetry fixes p₁, p₄, p₆, p₉. Each post’s trace (its intersection with {p₁, p₄, p₆, p₉}) is then preserved, which pins down which posts can move.
  • (b) Leaf sets. Each leaf set is a post minus the fixed points, or a union of posts with the same trace. So each leaf set is preserved as a whole.
  • (c) Restrictions. On each leaf set the symmetry lies in the matching factor: S₉ on the nine singletons, S₂ ≀ S₃ on the three pairs, S₄ on four accounts, S₂ on each two-account set.
  • (d) Product. Every symmetry is the product of its five restrictions, and each restriction lies in its factor. So every symmetry lies in H. With the lower bound, Aut(S₂₂) = H.

6. Rooms: the machine check

A room is a set of accounts with identical rows, meaning they sit on exactly the same posts. The 28 accounts form 20 rooms: one of size 4, five of size 2, and fourteen of size 1.

Caution on the next card. The numbers 768 = 2×384, 768 = 4×192 and 768 = 1×768 are the order of the time-ordered symmetry group, which also keeps the order of the posts. They are not the full group, which has order 1,672,151,040. Read them as a check of the room structure only.

7. Castings

A casting is a choice of seat: one room, or no monkey. That gives 21 castings. Up to symmetry they fall into 11 orbits: nine singleton rooms form one orbit, the three pair-rooms of L₂ form one orbit, L₃, L₄ and L₅ each give their own orbit, the five fixed points each give their own orbit, and no-monkey is the eleventh.

The earlier card that said “21 castings up to symmetry” is wrong. It counts one seat per room and does not merge symmetric seats. It is left out here.
