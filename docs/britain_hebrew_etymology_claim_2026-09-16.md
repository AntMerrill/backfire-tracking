## The actual etymology

**Britain** derives from Latin *Britannia*, itself from Ancient Greek
*Prettanikē*/*Brettanikē* (first attested via the explorer Pytheas,
~4th century BC), which comes from a Brittonic/Celtic root — *Pretani*
or *Priteni*. The leading scholarly reconstruction is that this meant
something like "the painted/tattooed people," describing the
inhabitants (compare Latin *Picti* → "Picts," literally "painted
ones," used for British tribes known for body painting/tattoos). It is
a Celtic word, transmitted through Greek and Latin — no Hebrew
etymology, root, or connection of any kind.

## What this claim actually is

"Britain means covenant land" / "has Hebrew roots" is not a fringe
one-off — it's a specific, named claim from **British Israelism**
(also "Anglo-Israelism"): a 19th-century pseudo-historical/religious
movement holding that Anglo-Saxon/British people are the literal
descendants of the "lost tribes of Israel." Its folk-etymology
reinterprets "Brit-ain" as Hebrew *brit* (covenant) + a suffix taken to
mean "land" or "people," yielding "Covenant Land." This has no support
in mainstream historical linguistics — it is a theological reading
invented to support the movement's genealogical claims, not a
linguistic finding.

## Verdict

**False.** The real etymology (Celtic → Greek → Latin) is well
documented and uncontested in historical linguistics. The "Hebrew
covenant" etymology is a known religious-ideological claim (British
Israelism), not a linguistic fact, and is not supported by any
mainstream etymological source.
