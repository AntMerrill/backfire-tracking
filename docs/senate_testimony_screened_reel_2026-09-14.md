# The screened reel inside the Senate testimony — compare/contrast

Companion to `senate_testimony_canoa_2026-09-14.md`. Covers what's actually in
the clip John pulled (01:47:14–01:50:20 of `youtube__-F9orJXMvR4`, the
watermarked file): not more of Ballard's spoken testimony, but a produced
short film the broadcast cuts to under an on-screen banner reading "Vídeo
exibido a pedido de convidado" ("Video shown at the guest's request").
Frame-sampled directly (2–8s intervals) across the full runtime to read the
title/credit cards, since they go by fast.

## What's on screen, in order

- **Dedication card** [01:48:10]: "This film is dedicated to the millions of
  children still trapped in the child trafficking system, awaiting rescue."
- **Stylized child close-up** [01:47:54], captioned "Acenda uma vela para que
  consiga ver no escuro" ("Light a candle so you can see in the dark") —
  shallow-focus, dramatically lit, cinematic B-roll style, not documentary
  footage.
- **"Wanted"-style mugshot card** [01:48:26]: two blurred-eye photos
  captioned "Leslie and Markhijn are being held on charges of child sex abuse
  material, suspected human trafficking, and child abuse. Both are expected
  to remain in prison until 2032." "Markhijn" reads as a Dutch name —
  connects to the "Dutch suspects" thread running through Video A's officer
  testimony and this same hearing's "holandês" reference, now with a name
  attached (unverified).
- **Intertitle** [~01:48:42]: "Aerial Recovery continues supporting the
  children of Canoa. Tim Ballard, Hristo Stoichkov, and the team recently
  surprised them with a special visit." (Confirmed by zooming a full-resolution
  capture — the low-res first pass misread this as "Krista Steinman.") First
  unambiguous on-screen spelling of "Canoa" (not "Canola") in this project.
  The name on screen is **Hristo Stoichkov** — the Bulgarian footballer
  (1994 Ballon d'Or, longtime Barça forward) — which the on-screen text
  claims took part in this visit. Not yet checked against any independent
  source for whether Stoichkov has any actual, verifiable association with
  Aerial Recovery, O.U.R., or Ballard.
- **Location card** [~01:48:52]: "CANOA, ECUADOR" over a waterfront/pier shot
  with palm trees, then a small-town street with shops and motorcycles —
  consistent in character with a small coastal town, matching the "no paved
  roads" profile flagged for Video B's street footage.
- **A dog in the back of a truck** [~01:49:00] — B-roll, no obvious
  connection to anything else in the project yet.
- **Gold-ball handout under a tent** [~01:49:16 onward]: a table event with
  Ballard and others, roughly ten gold-painted soccer balls lined up on the
  grass. On-screen caption, confirmed at full resolution: "Nunca paramos, a
  Aerial Recovery está aqui a cuidar destas crianças." ("We never stop,
  Aerial Recovery is here to take care of these children.") No children-count
  figure in this caption — an earlier low-resolution pass misread part of it
  as "100 crianças"; that reading doesn't hold up at full resolution and
  should be treated as wrong, not just unconfirmed.
  Interior room shot with children seated, separate from the tent scene.

- **Color correction** — Ricardo Dewaal ("Dewaal" — a second Dutch-sounding
  surname in this reel, alongside "Markhijn")

**No "starring"/cast card appears anywhere in the credits sequence** — this
reads as a documentary-style promotional reel built around Ballard, Hristo
Stoichkov, and unnamed children, not a scripted film with billed actors. That
matters for one specific thing: **John flagged that "Hidden War" stars Mel
Gibson "in this version."** Checked the full credits sequence frame by frame
(2-second intervals) and no Mel Gibson credit, or any cast list at all,
appears in this reel. Two possibilities, neither chased down yet: this reel
isn't the actual "Hidden War" film (the credits above may be for a separate
Aerial Recovery promo that happens to reuse the branding, same pattern as
everything else recurring across these sources), or a cast card exists
somewhere in the runtime this sampling pass missed. Flagging the mismatch
rather than writing the Gibson claim in as established — it isn't, on what's
been directly checked so far.

## Cross-references to the rest of the project

- **Matt Cooper**, credited here as a producer alongside Ballard, is the same
  name behind the [[project_matt_cooper_running_gag]] material elsewhere —
  worth cross-checking whether this is the same person.
- **Hristo Stoichkov**, named on the intertitle as personally taking part in
  the Canoa visit, is a famous, independently documented public figure
  — which makes this the single most easily checkable claim in this entire
  reel. Either there's a findable connection between Stoichkov and
  Aerial Recovery/O.U.R./Ballard, or there isn't, and that's answerable from
  outside sources rather than staying stuck as "flagged, not resolved."
- **Dutch-sounding names** now include: the raid clip's "eram holandeses"
  (Video A), the testimony's own "holandês" reference, and now two named
  credits in this reel — "Markhijn" (accused, mugshot card) and "Ricardo
  Dewaal" (color correction). Whether any of these are the same person, or
  correspond to identifiable individuals at all, is unchecked.
- **Children-count figures across the project, still three**: 220 (Senate
  testimony's own spoken figure), 20 children/200 videos (Argentina
  passage), 2 girls (Video A raid officer). This reel's own caption doesn't
  add a fourth number after all (see correction above) — none of the three
  are reconciled against each other.
- **Children's faces in this reel** — flagged by John as reading like
  AI-generated imagery rather than photographs, on direct viewing. Not run
  through reverse image search yet; see the artifact update for how that's
  currently phrased (flagged, not established).
