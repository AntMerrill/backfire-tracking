What the structure shows:

  • One host, assigned seats. All seven group posts were uploaded by latterdayavenger, which is not one of the 13. Ballard and Chad hold two fixed seats; the other three rotate.
  • The host is a regular. latterdayavenger is also credited on 42 other posts in Chad’s and C’s grids, May 31, 2025 to June 14, 2026, usually as one of three to five credited accounts.   • An old core. Before Ballard arrived there were already 25 links and 634 shared posts. Chad and C alone had 434. Chad–D, C–D and Chad–H were also established.
  • Thin new links. From Feb 18 the number of links nearly doubles, but most new ones are a single post (21 of 48). Accounts appear once or twice, then drop out. Part of that is arithmetic: one post with five credited accounts makes ten pairs at once (see the algebra below).

The seven posts as algebra

Not yet peer reviewed

The math in this section has not been reviewed by anyone outside our team. The counts come straight from the posts; the design-theory reading and the symmetry calculation are our own and may contain errors. Corrections are welcome. The matrix and the pair counts are on GitHub.

Treat the accounts as points and the seven group posts as blocks: 13 points, 7 blocks, 5 points in each block. That is an incidence structure, and its algebra says a lot about how the posts were put together.

The incidence matrix N. One row per account, one column per post; a filled cell means the account is credited on that post. Rows are grouped by symmetry (explained below). The right-hand column is r, the number of posts each account is on.

Key

Pairs come in bundles of ten. The pair counts are N·Nᵀ = ΣB 𝟙B𝟙Bᵀ, a sum over the posts. Each post with five credited accounts adds a complete set of ten pairs at once. So the lines in the maps above arrive ten at a time: that is the arithmetic of the format, not ten separate choices.

Key · each square is a pair of accounts, coloured by λ = how many of the seven posts they share (count of pairs in each band)

Deliberately unbalanced. In a balanced design every account would be on the same number of posts and every pair would meet equally often. Here r = 7 for Ballard and Chad and 1 to 4 for everyone else; the pair TB–LDC meets on all 7 posts, and only 40 of the 78 possible pairs ever meet at all.

Take out the fixed pair. Ballard and Chad are in every block, so remove them and look at what is left: seven triples on the other eleven accounts.

ABC · ACD · AED · ABD · FBG · HIJ · KGD

The first four triples all sit inside {A, B, C, D, E}, and A is in all four: a core. Then it branches, with B, D and G carrying over as bridges. HIJ stands on its own and shares no account with any other post.

Symmetry. The automorphism group Aut(N) is every way of relabelling the 13 accounts that carries the set of seven posts onto itself. Computed on these posts: |Aut(N)| = 12 ≅ S₂ × S₃, with orbits {TB, LDC} and {H, I, J}, and every other account fixed.

  • {TB, LDC}: from the group posts alone, swapping Ballard and Chad changes nothing. The format treats them as one unit, the two fixed seats. What tells them apart lies outside these posts: Ballard’s own December 20 post, and Chad’s 434 posts with C.
  • {H, I, J}: interchangeable. They appear once, together, as a whole block: a batch swapped in as a unit.
  • Fixed points: A, B, C, D, E, F, G and K each have a footprint no other account shares.

Large orbits mark interchangeable seat-fillers; fixed points mark accounts the structure depends on. This describes the posting pattern only, not who is behind any account. With latterdayavenger’s own grid captured, every post it hosts becomes another block, and the same calculation can be run on the whole design.

Check it yourself

Confirmed in SageMath 10.9 · Oct 5, 2026

The seven posts and the matrix N are published. Standard, free math software can compute the symmetry group on its own, without anything of ours:

D = IncidenceStructure(B)
G = D.automorphism_group()
G.order(), G.orbits()
We predicted order 12, with orbits {TB, LDC} and {H, I, J}, and every other account alone. SageMath 10.9 confirms it, as do networkx and our own count. If you get a different answer, tell us.

How we missed it

Instagram’s collaborator list on a post names the credited accounts but leaves out the account that posted it. On October 4 we counted Chad’s posts whose collaborator list named Ballard. A post Ballard made himself never names him, so the December 20 post didn’t count. It was in our captures of both accounts the whole time.

The fixed rule

A post ties two accounts if either one credits the other, or if the same post appears in both accounts’ grids. Recounted that way, Chad’s 1,463 captured posts before February 18 include exactly one tied to Ballard: December 20, 2025.

Limits: Chad’s posts come from two captures merged (October 1 and October 4, 2026), 1,888 posts reaching back to June 20, 2024. Ballard’s capture reaches back to September 11, 2025. The October 1 capture did not show Ballard’s credit even on the February group posts, likely because Ballard blocks the account we viewed it from, so it cannot fully rule out an earlier tie in Chad’s oldest posts (June 2024 to January 2025). Nothing before June 2024 is covered.
