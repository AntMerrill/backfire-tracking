Rooms: Gap Classes and the Holland View of a Posting Record

Adjunct to “A Rigid Grid and a Relay” and its sequel “A Hidden Section” · October 22, 2026 · preprint, not peer reviewed
Abstract. We give a way of grouping social-media posts and the accounts on them that uses structure only: posting times and co-posters, never content. An account's co-posts form a finite chain \(\Omega\); a gap threshold \(\tau\) cuts \(\Omega\) into convex gap classes (§1). On a class, choose points and an incidence; the rooms are the classes of equal rows (§2). Because a finite chain has \(A(\Omega)=1\) (Holland), the order-preserving symmetry of the class is exactly \[\operatorname{Aut}_{<}=\operatorname{Sym}(P_1)\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Sym}(P_r)\qquad(\text{Theorem 3.1}),\] which generalises Proposition 4.2 of the sequel from one grid to any class and any choice of points. Adding posts can only split rooms (§4). On Tim Ballard's Instagram for 10 October 2026 the co-poster group is \(C_2\); a word-incidence on the same class gives \(S_3\) (§5).

0Notation

We follow Glass [G]. \((\Omega,\le)\) is a chain and \(A(\Omega)\) the group of all order-preserving permutations of \(\Omega\) [H]. Permutations act on the right: \(\alpha g\), with \(\alpha(gh)=(\alpha g)h\). For \(G\le\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\), \(G_\alpha\) is the stabiliser of \(\alpha\) and \(\operatorname{supp}(g)=\{\alpha:\alpha g\ne\alpha\}\). A convex \(\Delta\subseteq\Omega\) is an o-block of \(G\) if \(\Delta g=\Delta\) or \(\Delta g\cap\Delta=\varnothing\) for every \(g\in G\). Co-posts of an account are the posts on which it is the owner or a listed collaborator (Definition 0.1 of the sequel).

Lemma 0.1. If \(\Omega\) is finite, \(A(\Omega)=1\).
\(g\in A(\Omega)\) sends the least element to the least element; remove it and repeat. ∎

1Gap classes

Definition 1.1. Let \(\Omega\) be finite, totally ordered by a posting time \(t:\Omega\to\mathbb R\), and \(\tau>0\). For consecutive \(\alpha<\beta\) write \(\alpha\mid\beta\) (a cut) iff \(t(\beta)-t(\alpha)>\tau\). The \(\tau\)-gap classes are the maximal intervals of \(\Omega\) containing no cut: \(\Omega=\Omega_1<\Omega_2<\cdots<\Omega_k\).
Proposition 1.2. The gap classes partition \(\Omega\) into convex sets, determined by \(t\) and \(\tau\) alone. Each is an o-block of \(A(\Omega)\).
The cuts are a set of adjacent pairs; removing them leaves intervals, which are convex and disjoint. By Lemma 0.1, \(A(\Omega)=1\), and every subset is an o-block of the trivial group. ∎

The second clause is cheap here and is stated only to fix language: when \(\Omega\) is infinite, or when a coarser order is used, o-blocks of a non-trivial group are the right generalisation of a gap class (§6). Membership in a class never depends on what a post says.

2Points, rows, rooms

Definition 2.1. Fix a class \(\Omega_i\), a finite set of points \(P\), and an incidence \(I\subseteq P\times\Omega_i\). The row of \(p\) is \(R(p)=\{\alpha\in\Omega_i:(p,\alpha)\in I\}\); the block of \(\alpha\) is \(B_\alpha=\{p:(p,\alpha)\in I\}\). The rooms are the classes of \(p\sim q\iff R(p)=R(q)\): \(P=P_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_r\).
Definition 2.2. \(\operatorname{Aut}(P,I)=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(P):\exists\,\pi\in\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega_i),\ (p,\alpha)\in I\Leftrightarrow(p\sigma,\alpha\pi)\in I\}\) (the unordered view), and \(\operatorname{Aut}_{<}(P,I)\) the same with \(\pi\in A(\Omega_i)\) (the Holland view).

Two incidences are used below: co-posters (\(P\) = accounts, \(I\) = co-posts; the object of the sequel) and words (\(P\) = a word list, \((p,\alpha)\in I\) iff \(p\) occurs in the post's transcript).

3The rooms theorem

Theorem 3.1. \(\operatorname{Aut}_{<}(P,I)=\operatorname{Sym}(P_1)\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Sym}(P_r)\). In particular \(|\operatorname{Aut}_{<}|=\prod_j|P_j|!\) and \(\operatorname{Aut}_{<}\le\operatorname{Aut}\).
By Lemma 0.1 the only admissible \(\pi\) is the identity, so \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{<}\) iff \((p,\alpha)\in I\Leftrightarrow (p\sigma,\alpha)\in I\) for all \(p,\alpha\), i.e. \(R(p\sigma)=R(p)\) for all \(p\). That says exactly that \(\sigma\) preserves each room, and any permutation preserving each room does so. The inclusion holds because \(\pi=1\) is allowed in \(\operatorname{Aut}\). ∎
Remark 3.2. This is Proposition 4.2 of the sequel (“seats, not days”) with \(\mathcal S_h^{+}\) replaced by any gap class and any incidence. The index \([\operatorname{Aut}:\operatorname{Aut}_{<}]\) measures the symmetry that exists only if posts are allowed to change places in time.

The answer is the rooms. A room is a set of points the record cannot tell apart.

4Monotonicity

Proposition 4.1. Let \(\Omega_i\subseteq\Omega'\) (more posts, same points, \(I\subseteq I'\) agreeing on \(\Omega_i\)). Every room of \((P,I')\) lies inside a room of \((P,I)\). Hence \(\operatorname{Aut}_{<}(P,I')\le\operatorname{Aut}_{<}(P,I)\).
If \(R'(p)=R'(q)\) over \(\Omega'\), intersecting with \(\Omega_i\) gives \(R(p)=R(q)\). The groups follow from Theorem 3.1. ∎

So a later capture, or a wider class, can only split a room, never create one: the same one-way property as the owner correction in the sequel (§3 there). A room found on a small class is a claim that survives only as long as no new post separates its members.

5The set of 10 October 2026

\(\Omega\) = co-posts of timballard89, 10–14 October 2026 (capture of 17 October, US Pacific time), 22 posts. With \(\tau=10\) h the classes have sizes \(8,4,5,5\) (cuts of 15.9 h, 10.8 h, 28.6 h). \(\Omega_1\), all eight posts of 10 October, has a 37.6 h gap before it and 15.9 h after; we call it THE SET and write \(P_1<\cdots<P_8\) for its posts.

Proposition 5.0 (the class is not a cut-off). For a class \(\Omega_i\) with longest inner gap \(g_i\) and boundary gaps \(b_i^-\), \(b_i^+\), \(\Omega_i\) is a \(\tau\)-gap class exactly for \(\tau\in[g_i,\min(b_i^-,b_i^+))\). For \(\Omega_1\): \(g_1=5.7\) h, \(b_1^-=37.6\) h, \(b_1^+=15.9\) h, so every \(\tau\in[5.7,15.9)\) h gives the same eight posts.
No inner pair is cut iff \(\tau\ge g_i\); both boundary pairs are cut iff \(\tau<\min(b_i^-,b_i^+)\). ∎
Definition 5.0′ (birth, death, persistence). As \(\tau\) increases from \(0\), cuts disappear in order of gap size and classes merge; the classes over all \(\tau\) form a nested hierarchy (single-linkage clustering of \(t(\Omega)\subset\mathbb R\)). A class \(\Omega_i\) is born at \(\beta_i=g_i\) and dies at \(\delta_i=\min(b_i^-,b_i^+)\) (Proposition 5.0). Its persistence is measured either by width \(\delta_i-\beta_i\) (hours) or by ratio \(\delta_i/\beta_i\) (scale-free). A class is more canonical than another if it persists longer in the chosen measure; the measure is fixed before the data are read.

Proposition 5.1 (co-posters). With \(T,d,m\) = timballard89, druze.spirit, manar_morshed_official: \(B_{P_3}=\{T,d,m\}\) and \(B_{P_j}=\{T\}\) for \(j\ne3\). The rooms are \(\{T\},\{d,m\}\), and \(\operatorname{Aut}_{<}=\operatorname{Aut}=\langle(d\,m)\rangle\cong C_2\).

Example 5.2 (words). With a nine-word seed list (Catherine, God, Utah, Israel, Isaiah, Backfire, forgive, war, children) matched against Whisper transcripts, the rooms of size \(>1\) are \(\{\text{Catherine},\text{Backfire},\text{forgive}\}\), all with row \(\{P_8\}\); so \(\operatorname{Aut}_{<}\cong S_3\). Unordered, \(\text{God}\leftrightarrow\text{children}\) with \(P_5\leftrightarrow P_6\) also acts, and \(\operatorname{Aut}\cong S_3\times C_2\) of order 12 (computed in Sage (Opie), checked by brute force over \(9!\)).

The word list was a seed, never chosen; Example 5.2 illustrates Theorem 3.1 and is not a finding. The co-poster incidence is the one the sequel's object uses.

6Gap classes as o-blocks

Whether Glass's o-blocks recover the gap classes is a question about infinite chains, and it has its own answer, proved separately in Appendix A: the \(\tau\)-classes are o-blocks of the gap-preserving subgroup \(G_\tau\le A(\Omega)\) (Theorem A.2), and every non-trivial o-block through an interior point of a class contains that class (Theorem A.3).

Assumption. From here on \(\Omega\) is finite.

Then \(G_\tau\le A(\Omega)=1\) (Lemma 0.1), every convex set is an o-block, and the order prefers no partition. Every class of a finite record has a first and a last post, so Theorem A.3 says nothing at the endpoints, which is exactly where a session's last post and the next session's first post can share an o-block across a cut. Canonicity of a finite class therefore comes from the times (Definition 5.0′), not from the group.

7The canonical partition and the null

Proposition 7.1. For a finite run, the partition into \(\tau\)-classes is constant for \(\tau\in[\max_i g_i,\ \min c)\), where \(c\) ranges over the cut gaps. For 10–14 October this is \([5.7,10.8)\) h: every \(\tau\) there gives \(8\mid4\mid5\mid5\).
No inner pair is cut iff \(\tau\ge\max_i g_i\); every cut survives iff \(\tau<\min c\). ∎

We propose choosing \(\tau\) in the widest valley of the log-gap distribution, and preferring the ratio measure of Definition 5.0′ with a minimum class size (tiny classes are born near \(0\) and have inflated ratios).

Observation 7.2 (the null). Over the whole 17 October capture of timballard89 (3,684 posts, 1,852 classes at \(\tau=10\) h), 41 of the 81 classes with at least eight posts have a room of size \(\ge2\); so do 157 of the 438 classes with at least two posts. 11.7% of posts carry two or more co-posters besides timballard89, and any such post whose guests appear nowhere else in their class makes a room.

A room in one class is common. A room that recurs is not.

So Proposition 5.1 is not, by itself, evidence of anything. What would be: the same room (the same accounts with equal rows) recurring across many classes, or rooms larger than any single group post explains.

8Questions

  1. Over a longer run, does the widest log-gap valley select the most persistent classes, and do width and ratio then agree?
  2. How often does a given room recur across classes, against the null of Observation 7.2?
  3. Theorem A.3 assumes o-primitivity on each class. What is the weakest hypothesis under which the classes are still the minimal o-blocks?

AAppendix: the infinite case

Here \(\Omega\) may be infinite. The results and proofs in this appendix are claude's (October 22, 2026), unreviewed.

Definition A.1. Let \(t:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) be strictly increasing and \(\tau>0\). Write \(\alpha\sim\beta\) iff \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are joined by a chain \(\alpha=\gamma_0<\gamma_1<\cdots<\gamma_n=\beta\) (or the same with \(\beta<\alpha\)) in \(\Omega\) with \(t(\gamma_{j+1})-t(\gamma_j)\le\tau\). The \(\tau\)-classes are the \(\sim\)-classes; they are convex, and for finite \(\Omega\) they are the gap classes of Definition 1.1. Put \[G_\tau=\{g\in A(\Omega):\ \alpha\sim\beta\iff\alpha g\sim\beta g\ \text{ for all }\alpha,\beta\in\Omega\}.\]
Theorem A.2 (claude). Every \(\tau\)-class is an o-block of \(G_\tau\); the class partition is a convex \(G_\tau\)-congruence.
Let \(\Delta\) be a class and \(g\in G_\tau\). If \(\alpha,\beta\in\Delta\) then \(\alpha g\sim\beta g\), so \(\Delta g\) lies in one class. If \(\gamma\sim\alpha g\) with \(\alpha\in\Delta\), then \(\gamma g^{-1}\sim\alpha\) (the defining condition is an equivalence, so \(g^{-1}\) also preserves \(\sim\)); hence \(\gamma\in\Delta g\), and \(\Delta g\) is a whole class. Classes partition \(\Omega\), so \(\Delta g=\Delta\) or \(\Delta g\cap\Delta=\varnothing\). ∎

The converse fails without a hypothesis: for finite \(\Omega\), \(A(\Omega)=1\) and every convex set is an o-block.

Theorem A.3 (claude). Let \(\Delta\) be a \(\tau\)-class whose setwise stabiliser \(H=G_{\tau,\{\Delta\}}\) acts o-primitively on \(\Delta\) [G]. Call \(\alpha\in\Delta\) interior if \(\Delta\) has points both above and below \(\alpha\). Then every o-block of \(G_\tau\) with more than one point that contains an interior point of \(\Delta\) contains \(\Delta\).
Let \(\Gamma\) be such an o-block, \(\alpha\in\Gamma\cap\Delta\) interior. (i) \(\Gamma\cap\Delta\) is an o-block of \(H\) on \(\Delta\): it is convex, and \((\Gamma\cap\Delta)h=\Gamma h\cap\Delta\) equals \(\Gamma\cap\Delta\) or misses it. (ii) By o-primitivity, \(\Gamma\cap\Delta=\{\alpha\}\) or \(\Delta\). (iii) Suppose \(\Gamma\cap\Delta=\{\alpha\}\); take \(\beta\in\Gamma\setminus\{\alpha\}\), say \(\beta>\alpha\). As \(\beta\notin\Delta\) and \(\Delta\) is convex, \(\beta\) lies above \(\Delta\); as \(\alpha\) is interior there is \(\delta\in\Delta\) with \(\alpha<\delta<\beta\), and \(\delta\in\Gamma\) by convexity, a contradiction (\(\beta<\alpha\) is symmetric). So \(\Gamma\supseteq\Delta\). ∎
Corollary A.4. If no \(\tau\)-class has a least or a greatest element and each stabiliser acts o-primitively, the \(\tau\)-classes are exactly the minimal o-blocks of \(G_\tau\) with more than one point. (Every point is interior; combine A.2 and A.3.)
Remark A.5 (endpoints). The interior hypothesis cannot be dropped. If \(\alpha\) is the last point of a class and \(\beta\) the first point of the next, with nothing between, and \(G_\tau\) fixes both (as on any finite record), then \(\{\alpha,\beta\}\) is an o-block that straddles a cut and contains no class.
Remark A.6. When posting is “continuous” within each session (e.g. times dense in open intervals separated by gaps \(>\tau\)), the group recovers the sessions on its own. For a finite record every class has endpoints, Theorem A.3 is silent at the endpoints, and \(G_\tau=1\): canonicity must come from the times (Definition 5.0′); see §6. If a single class is all of \(\mathbb R\), Corollary A.4 returns \(\Omega\) itself, consistent with \(A(\mathbb R)\) being o-primitive.
