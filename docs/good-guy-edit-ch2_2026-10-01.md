## His claims about Gerard, sorted

**What he says he knows: nothing first-hand.** "I've never met this person. He's never said a word to me." (0:17)

**How sure he sounds, by minute:**

- 0:38 — Gerard's voice "would have been listened to … and in this case, clearly was rejected." Stated as fact.
- 0:53 — "I assume he was in that capacity or something like that." An assumption.
- 1:09 — "I only could imagine that he fought for me." Imagination.
- 1:30 — "he would have stood up for me I think he did." A belief.
- 2:31 — "I don't know what his intent was, but we'll find out through discovery." He doesn't know.

The certainty runs backwards. The firmest claim, that Gerard's voice was "clearly … rejected," comes first. The basis, "I only could imagine," comes after it.

**Compare Chapter 1 (Sept 22):** "I don't know if you sided with Jack Gerard or not," and "like Jack Gerard, I don't think agreed." So within three weeks Gerard goes from a man who "fought for me" (Sept 4) to one Ballard doesn't know about (Sept 22). The Facebook re-post on Sept 30 puts the Sept 4 version back out.

**His job title shifts within one minute.** At 0:38 Gerard is "executive director of the church communication department." By 0:53 he is "put in as the PR director … or something like that." The Sept 22 overlay says "former Director Church Communications (2023)." None of them cites a source.

**"Swept away to Europe."** Ballard makes the same move for both sides: Kevin Pearson (whom he blames) was "moved to Europe" (Backfire, 1:10:05), and Gerard (whom he praises) was "swept away to Europe." Then: "Like Kevin Pearson for bad or Jack Gerard for good. I guess that's where you go." So being sent to Europe gets used as evidence in both directions, as punishment for the bad Kevin and as exile for the good watchman.

**The talk.** He gets the title right: "Integrity: A Christlike Attribute," April 2024. He says it was given "just months after the decision was made," and frames it as aimed at the 2023 decision: "I think he's talking about what happened just months earlier." Nothing in the excerpt he plays names Ballard, OUR, a meeting or a decision (same finding as Chapter 1). How he reorders it is covered below.

## The excerpt, against the published talk

Source: Elder Jack N. Gerard, Of the Seventy, "Integrity: A Christlike Attribute," April 2024 general conference. https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2024/04/17gerard?lang=eng (retrieved 2026-10-01).

**The sentences play in a different order from the talk.** In the published talk the order is:

1. "…some organizations train employees to consider what their decisions or decision-making process would look like if published online or on the front page of a major newspaper."
2. "As the Church comes out of obscurity and darkness, we, like Daniel, must rise above the worldly expectations…"
3. "As a covenant people, and as leaders of His Church, we must be beyond reproach and aligned with the standards the Lord has set."
4. "Acting with integrity builds faith and trust and reassures others that we seek only to do the will of the Lord."
5. *"In our councils, we resist outside influences and follow the Lord's revealed process, seeking insights from each woman and man and acting consistent with the inspired counsel received."*
6. "Our focus is on the Savior, and we are careful to avoid actions that may be perceived as serving our own interests, benefiting our family, or favoring someone at the expense of another."
7. "We go out of our way to avoid any perception that our actions may be influenced by the honors of men, to receive personal recognition, generate more likes, be quoted or published."

Ballard's Episode 7 plays them as **3 → 4 → 2 → 6 → 7 → 1**:

- **The "front page of a major newspaper" line moves from first to last.** In the talk it opens the passage. In Ballard's cut it closes it, and he follows it straight away with "I think he's talking about what happened just months earlier."
- **Sentence 5 is left out.** It's the one that says Church councils "follow the Lord's revealed process," which cuts against Ballard's story of a council that ignored it.
- **Also trimmed:** "like Daniel," "benefiting our family," and "to receive personal recognition, generate more likes, be quoted or published." The last one is the part that makes "honors of men" about personal vanity.

**"He says …" after the clip ends (4:11).** "He says, the decisions you make and the process you use to make a decision assume that someday that will be headline. And will you be embarrassed?" This comes in Ballard's own voice, after Gerard's footage stops. Those sentences aren't in the published talk. The nearest thing is near the start of the talk, where Gerard recalls a question Elder Uchtdorf asked him in an interview while reorganizing his stake: "Has there been anything in your life that, if brought to the attention of the public, would be an embarrassment to you or the Church?" That is a question about Gerard's own life, not about a Church decision.

**"Prophetic, except he got one thing wrong."** After building Gerard up as the righteous watchman, Ballard corrects him: it won't just be headlines, it will be "Sound of Freedom 2 … a docuseries … an international bestselling book." So the Good Guy's talk becomes a promo for Ballard's own projects.

## The other Kevin story (from the full Backfire video)

Episode 7 names Kevin Pearson only in passing ("for bad"). The story behind it is told at **2:41:54–2:44:01** of the September 4 Backfire video, and it's hard to follow. In order, as he tells it:

1. "The person who Kevin Pearson sent [to do the] excommunication is a man named Kevin Hamilton." (2:41:54)
2. "He wasn't in the area authority so they have plausible deniability." Then: "I believe he's the one who instructed the state president" (2:42:05–2:42:11)
3. "has Kevin Hamilton ever expressed his opinions about this?" (2:42:22)
4. "So Kevin Hamilton, he's the one that Kevin Pearson purportedly ordered to come do the hit job. He's not exactly in the area authority he was working in the genealogy department I believe." (2:42:37)
5. "After he orders the hit job probably tells the state president don't worry about [D&C] 102" (2:42:55)
6. "He goes to my wife at State Conference … The very state conference where the state president was recently called and then his first order of business given after they told me this. After Kevin Hamilton set him apart, he said, you know, go do this to Tim Ballard." (2:43:08–2:43:28)
7. "He finds my wife in the congregation and [Kevin / Catherine] said he was really nice, once you know the background, kind of creepy. He says, you're going to need the Savior right now, Sister Ballard … It's going to be a bumpy ride." (2:43:28–2:43:46)

**Where it doesn't hang together:**

- **Who ordered whom changes mid-story.** In (1) Pearson "sent" Hamilton. In (4) Pearson "purportedly ordered" him. In (5) it's Hamilton who "orders the hit job." Then in (6) Hamilton sets apart a brand-new stake president whose "first order of business" is "go do this to Tim Ballard."
- **The qualifiers stack up.** "I believe … purportedly … I believe … probably." By step 5 every link in the chain is hedged, yet step 6 ("go do this to Tim Ballard") is told as a quote.
- **"[Kevin / Catherine] said he was really nice."** He slurs the name. Every stored transcript (the corrected master SRT and scene clip 548) hears **"Kevin."** A re-run with Whisper `small` hears "Catherine," his wife, which is presumably what he means. Either way it doesn't fit. If it's "Kevin," a Kevin is vouching for the Kevin he just accused. If it's "Catherine," his wife found the man "really nice" at the time, and Ballard re-labels it "creepy" only "once you know the background," a background that, by his own wording, he "believe[s]" and calls "purported." Clip: `workup/ear_kevin_said_02-43-22.wav`, about 11–12 seconds in.
- **The scene.** The new stake president's setting apart, Hamilton's instruction, and Hamilton working the congregation to find Sister Ballard all happen at one stake conference. Ballard isn't there by his own account, yet he reports Hamilton's private words to the new stake president.
- **"State president."** Ballard says "state president" throughout for *stake* president, and "State Conference" for stake conference. Quoted as spoken (see the No-sane-washing rule).
