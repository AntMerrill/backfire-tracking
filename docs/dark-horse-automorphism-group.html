Abstract. We model the 22 Instagram co-posts of May 1 to June 30, 2025 in which \(p_{1}\) shares credit, the “Dark Horse collaboration”, as a finite incidence structure \(\mathcal S_{22}\) on \(v=28\) accounts: accounts are points, posts are blocks, and one block occurs four times. We prove \[\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S_{22})\;\cong\;S_9\times(S_2\wr S_3)\times S_4\times S_2\times S_2,\] of order \(2^{16}\,3^{6}\,5\cdot7=1{,}672{,}151{,}040\approx1.67\times10^{9}\), with 10 orbits: five fixed points and five nontrivial orbits. Unlike the seven group posts of the first paper, where the group came from twins, every nontrivial orbit here is a pendant star, and the star centres are nested: \(\{p_1\}\subset\{p_1,p_4\}\subset\{p_1,p_4,p_6\}\). The upper bound is proved by hand from replication numbers and block traces; the lower bound by the pendant-star lemma; SageMath 10.9 confirms both.

1Setting

Definition 1.1. An incidence structure is \(\mathcal S=(P,\mathcal B)\) with \(P\) a finite set of points and \(\mathcal B\) a finite multiset of subsets of \(P\) (blocks). Its incidence matrix \(N\in\{0,1\}^{P\times\mathcal B}\) has \(N_{x,B}=1\) iff \(x\in B\). The automorphism group is \[\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(P):\ \sigma(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B\ \text{as multisets}\},\] equivalently the \(\sigma\) for which some permutation matrix \(Q\) gives \(P_\sigma N Q = N\).

The Levi (incidence) graph \(L(\mathcal S)\) is bipartite on \(P\sqcup\mathcal B\), with \(x\sim B\) iff \(x\in B\). Colour-preserving automorphisms of \(L\), restricted to \(P\), are exactly \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)\); this is how Sage computes it. A repeated block is two vertices of \(L\) with the same neighbourhood.

Data. Points are accounts; a block is the set of accounts on one post: those Instagram credits as collaborators, plus the account whose grid the post came from. The 22 posts are every co-post of May 1 to June 30, 2025 (UTC) with \(p_1\) among its accounts, as captured from \(p_1\)’s grid on October 17, 2026 and published as posts.csv.

Since block sizes vary, \(\mathcal S_{22}\) is not a \(t\)-design for any \(t\). The Levi graph has \(28+22=50\) vertices and \(\sum_B|B|=56\) edges.

A multiset. \(B_3=B_6=B_8=B_{15}=\{p_1,p_6\}\): the same two-point block occurs four times. Definition 1.1 counts it four times, so \(b=22\), and an automorphism must send a block of multiplicity 4 to a block of multiplicity 4. Here that costs nothing, because \(p_1\) and \(p_6\) turn out to be fixed anyway (Lemma 3.3).

Definition 2.1. Points \(x,y\) are twins if their rows of \(N\) agree: \(x\) and \(y\) lie on exactly the same blocks.
Lemma 2.2. If \(T\) is a twin class, \(\operatorname{Sym}(T)\le\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)\), acting trivially off \(T\).
A permutation of \(T\) fixes every block setwise, since each block contains all of \(T\) or none of it. □

\(\mathcal S_{22}\) has six twin classes of size \(>1\): \(\{p_{25},p_{26},p_{27},p_{28}\}\) (all on \(B_{22}\) only) and the pairs \(\{p_3,p_5\}\) (\(B_2\)), \(\{p_{10},p_{11}\}\) (\(B_9\)), \(\{p_{12},p_{13}\}\) (\(B_{11}\)), \(\{p_{18},p_{19}\}\) (\(B_{17}\)), \(\{p_{23},p_{24}\}\) (\(B_{21}\)). By Lemma 2.2 they contribute \(S_4\times S_2^{\,5}\), of order \(24\cdot32=768\). That is the whole group if blocks must be fixed setwise, which is what the 11-02 count of 768 for this structure measured; the full group is \(2{,}177{,}280=9!\cdot3!\) times larger, and the next lemma says where the factor comes from.

3Pendant stars with nested centres

Lemma 3.1 (pendant stars). Let \(C\subseteq P\) and let \(T_1,\dots,T_m\) be pairwise disjoint \(k\)-subsets of \(P\setminus C\) such that \(C\cup T_i\in\mathcal B\) for each \(i\), and no point of \(T=\bigcup T_i\) lies on any other block. Then \(S_k\wr S_m\le\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)\), acting on \(T\) imprimitively with block system \(\{T_1,\dots,T_m\}\) and trivially off \(T\).
Each \(T_i\) is a twin class, so the base group \(\prod_i\operatorname{Sym}(T_i)\cong S_k^{\,m}\) lies in \(\operatorname{Aut}\) by Lemma 2.2. For \(\pi\in S_m\), fix bijections \(T_i\to T_{\pi(i)}\) and let \(\hat\pi\) act by them on \(T\) and trivially elsewhere; \(\hat\pi\) maps \(C\cup T_i\) to \(C\cup T_{\pi(i)}\) and fixes every other block, so \(\hat\pi\in\operatorname{Aut}\). Base group and these lifts generate \(S_k\wr S_m\). □

Unlike Theorem 3.2 of the first paper, which needed Sage to show that the orbit groups multiply to \(|G|\), nothing here depends on a machine: the trace argument closes the gap by hand. Sage’s role (Section 4) is to check the hand proof, not to replace it. One name differs: Sage reports the six-point factor as C2 x S4, which is the same group, since \(S_2\wr S_3\cong C_2\times S_4\) (the symmetry group of the cube, of order 48, acting on its three axes with a sign on each); so Sage’s structure_description() for the whole group reads C2 x C2 x C2 x S9 x S4 x S4.

Orbit-stabilizer. \(|G|=|\operatorname{Orb}(x)|\,|\operatorname{Stab}(x)|\) for every point (Gallian, Ch. 7). The stabilizer of a leaf is the whole group except on its own star, where it is the stabilizer in that factor:

Burnside. The number of orbits is \(\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|\operatorname{Fix}(g)|\). Summing over \(1.67\times10^9\) elements by hand is out of the question, but for a direct product acting on a disjoint union the sum splits over the factors, \[\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|\operatorname{Fix}(g)|=\sum_{O}\frac1{|G^O|}\sum_{h\in G^O}|\operatorname{Fix}_O(h)|=\sum_O\#\{\text{orbits of }G^O\text{ on }O\},\] and each factor is transitive: \(5+5=10\). The one factor worth doing by hand is \(S_2\wr S_3\) on its six leaves, by the cycle type of the top permutation \(\pi\in S_3\) and the number of trivial base coordinates:

Reading. In \(\mathcal S_{22}\) the structure sees five accounts as individuals: \(p_1\) (on every block), \(p_6\) (five posts, four of them the same two-point block), \(p_4\) (three posts, two of them with other guests), \(p_9\) (two posts) and \(p_{21}\) (fixed only because its one post is \(p_9\)’s). The other 23 accounts are leaves: within a star they are interchangeable (twins), and the stars on \(\{p_1\}\) are interchangeable across posts (the top group of the wreath product). Nine of the 22 posts are \(p_1\) plus one one-off guest. The first paper found the same mechanism on 492 posts; here it is the whole story, and the twin-driven \(D_6\) of the seven group posts does not recur. “Same orbit” means identical position in this incidence structure, nothing more.

Replication of the data. The mathematics is only as good as the incidence matrix. Anyone can rebuild \(N\) for \(\mathcal S_{22}\) from Instagram itself:

  1. open the 22 posts (links in posts.csv);
  2. record each post’s uploader and its Collaborators list; number the accounts by first appearance, alphabetically within a post, with \(p_1\) the account on every block;
  3. run the Sage calls of Section 4 on the resulting blocks.

The test needs no knowledge of our labels, because \(|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)|\) and the orbit sizes are invariant under relabelling the points. The Caucus page’s README gives the capture method and its limits (posts can be deleted or lose collaborators after a capture).

p8: prediction vs Sage

John’s question was what had been predicted for \(p_8\). The prediction was sealed at 20:12, before Sage ran (it is the file predict.md): it put \(p_8\) in the nine-point star centred on \(\{p_1\}\), the factor \(S_9\) of order \(9!=362{,}880\), on the nine one-guest blocks \(B_1,B_4,B_5,B_{12},B_{13},B_{14},B_{16},B_{18},B_{20}\), whose leaves are \(\{p_2,p_7,p_8,p_{14},p_{15},p_{16},p_{17},p_{20},p_{22}\}\). Predicted, before Sage: orbit \(S_9\) of order 362,880 on nine points, containing \(p_8\).

Checked after Sage (against sage_results.json): the orbit of \(p_8\) is \(\{p_2,p_7,p_8,p_{14},p_{15},p_{16},p_{17},p_{20},p_{22}\}\), of size 9, with induced group of order 362,880 and description S9; inside that factor the stabilizer is \(S_8\), so \(9\cdot40{,}320=362{,}880\), while the stabilizer in \(G\) has order 185,794,560 \(=|G|/9\). The set, the group and the order are the predicted ones. Match: yes.

Blocks. The star sits on \(B_1,B_4,B_5,B_{12},B_{13},B_{14},B_{16},B_{18},B_{20}\); \(p_8\) itself lies on one of them, \(B_5\). Nothing in this paragraph was known when the prediction was written: the prediction is the sealed file, the Sage output the check; no part was adjusted afterwards.

6Scope

The structure is only as complete as the capture of October 17, 2026, of \(p_1\)’s grid; a co-post removed before that date is not a block. “Same orbit” means identical position in this incidence structure, not common control, and the paper says nothing about who operates any account. The proofs in Section 3 are by hand and complete; the Sage session is a check. The paper has not been refereed by mathematicians. Corrections are welcome.
