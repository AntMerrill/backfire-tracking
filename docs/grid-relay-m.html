Only the rigid grid with relays has an m

One rule, run on nine co-post tables. Three of them have an m, and all three have the shape of the rigid grid and its relay. The other six have none, including Tim Ballard with druze.spirit.

Definitions

definitionA co-post table: posts as columns, credited accounts (owner and listed collaborators) as rows. Caption tags are not credits.

definitionFrame: the accounts on every post. Regulars: the other accounts on 2 or more posts.

definitionm: the post owner whose own posts contain every post each other regular is credited on (if several, the one owning most).

definitionMonkey: a human operator. Robot: any account other than m, a role in the structure, not a statement about who or what runs an account.

Results

computedThree of the nine tables have an m: S16, the first 16 co-posts of timballard89 and sandrabronzina (m = expofamily.cl, owner of posts 11–15); LDA's grid of 354 posts (m = latterdayavenger, owner of all 354); and the 7 Chadverse group posts (m = latterdayavenger, owner of all 7).

computedOnly in S16 does m also seat the regulars evenly: the other three meet pairwise 2, 2, 2 times on m's posts. In the grid and the relay m owns every post, so holding every regular comes free there, and the meeting counts are not equal.

computedIn S16 the account owning the most posts is sandrabronzina (6), and her posts hold none of the regulars. Counting owners alone picks the wrong account.

computedNo m in the other six: Tim Ballard × druze.spirit (36 posts), druze.spirit's whole timeline (200 posts), the Mario Frias co-posters, Ballard × Bolsonaro, the name-tag test and Holland order 2.

computedTim Ballard × druze.spirit: both are on all 36 posts. Tim owns 18, druze.spirit 16, tbfrescue 2. Each side's posts hold 2 of the 6 regulars.

computeddruze.spirit's timeline: 108 accounts, 200 co-posts. druze.spirit owns 118 and is on all 200, and its posts hold 11 of the 47 other regulars. timballard89 owns 8 and holds 3 of 46.

from the papersThe three tables with an m share one shape: one account nothing else can stand in for, a rigid recurring part, and all remaining symmetry from forced or one-post rooms. That is the rigid grid and its relay in P1, and the hub in P2.

provedAt most one monkey in any of these tables (see the finding page).

not claimedWhether m is a robot, or who or what runs any account.
