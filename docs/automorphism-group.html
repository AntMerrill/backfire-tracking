Automorphism Group of the Incidence Structure and Its Orbits

On the collaborator network of the timballard89 Instagram account · October 6, 2026 · not yet peer reviewed

Dedicated to the memory of Dean Neumann.

“When you’re the smartest guy in the room, you don’t need to say so.”

Abstract. We model Instagram collaboration credits as a finite incidence structure: accounts are points, posts are blocks. For the seven “group posts” of February to April 2026 we show \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S_7)\cong S_2\times S_3\cong D_6\), with orbits \(\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\) and \(\{p_{10},p_{11},p_{12}\}\), and verify the orbit-stabilizer theorem and Burnside’s lemma on it. For all 492 posts in which \(p_{1}\) shares credit we prove \[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S_{492})\;\cong\;&(S_2\wr S_7)\times S_{11}\times(S_3\wr S_3)\times(S_4\wr S_2)\\&\times(S_2\wr S_2)^2\times S_4\times S_3^{\,3}\times S_2^{\,18},\end{aligned}\] of order \(2^{60}\,3^{16}\,5^{3}\,7^{2}\,11\approx3.34\times10^{30}\), with 73 fixed points and 28 nontrivial orbits. The wreath factors come from one mechanism, the pendant star: posts consisting of \(p_{1}\) and one-off guests. All computations are in SageMath 10.9.

1Setting

Definition 1.1. An incidence structure is \(\mathcal S=(P,\mathcal B)\) with \(P\) a finite set of points and \(\mathcal B\) a finite multiset of subsets of \(P\) (blocks). Its incidence matrix \(N\in\{0,1\}^{P\times\mathcal B}\) has \(N_{x,B}=1\) iff \(x\in B\). The automorphism group is \[\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(P):\ \sigma(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B\ \text{as multisets}\},\] equivalently the \(\sigma\) for which some permutation matrix \(Q\) gives \(P_\sigma N Q = N\).

The Levi (incidence) graph \(L(\mathcal S)\) is bipartite on \(P\sqcup\mathcal B\), with \(x\sim B\) iff \(x\in B\). Colour-preserving automorphisms of \(L\), restricted to \(P\), are exactly \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)\); this is how Sage computes it (canonical labelling of a coloured graph).

Data. Points are accounts; a block is the set of accounts on one post: those Instagram credits as collaborators, plus the account whose grid the post came from (Instagram omits the uploader from the credit list). Points are written \(p_1,\dots,p_{13}\) (labels "p1"…"p13" in Sage); the plain-English pages use letters instead. The translation is in the notation block above.

Definition 2.1. Points \(x,y\) are twins if their rows of \(N\) agree: \(x\) and \(y\) lie on exactly the same blocks.
Lemma 2.2. If \(T\) is a twin class, \(\operatorname{Sym}(T)\le\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)\), acting trivially off \(T\).
A permutation of \(T\) fixes every block setwise, since each block contains all of \(T\) or none of it. □
Proposition 2.3. \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S_7)=\operatorname{Sym}\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\times\operatorname{Sym}\{p_{10},p_{11},p_{12}\}\cong S_2\times S_3\cong D_6\), of order 12.
The twin classes of size \(>1\) are \(\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\) (both on every block) and \(\{p_{10},p_{11},p_{12}\}\) (all on \(B_6\) only), so by Lemma 2.2 the group contains \(S_2\times S_3\). 3All 492 collaborations

Let \(\mathcal S_{492}\) have as blocks every captured post on which \(p_{1}\) and at least one other account appear (492 posts; 74 caption-only mentions excluded), on \(v=172\) points, block sizes 2 to 5. Sage finds \(|G|=3{,}343{,}786{,}911{,}907{,}539{,}166{,}867{,}488{,}768{,}000=2^{60}3^{16}5^{3}7^{2}11\) and 101 orbits: 73 fixed points and 28 nontrivial orbits containing 99 points. There are 33 twin classes (25 of size 2, six of size 3, two of size 4).

Lemma 3.1 (pendant stars). Let \(C\subseteq P\) and let \(T_1,\dots,T_m\) be pairwise disjoint \(k\)-subsets of \(P\setminus C\) such that \(C\cup T_i\in\mathcal B\) for each \(i\), and no point of \(T=\bigcup T_i\) lies on any other block. Then \(S_k\wr S_m\le\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S)\), acting on \(T\) imprimitively with block system \(\{T_1,\dots,T_m\}\) and trivially off \(T\).
Each \(T_i\) is a twin class, so the base group \(\prod_i\operatorname{Sym}(T_i)\cong S_k^{\,m}\) lies in \(\operatorname{Aut}\) by Lemma 2.2. For \(\pi\in S_m\), fix bijections \(T_i\to T_{\pi(i)}\) and let \(\hat\pi\) act by them on \(T\) and trivially elsewhere; \(\hat\pi\) maps \(C\cup T_i\) to \(C\cup T_{\pi(i)}\) and fixes every other block, so \(\hat\pi\in\operatorname{Aut}\). Base group and these lifts generate \(S_k\wr S_m\). □

Remark. One of us has called Lemma 3.1 the “pedant-star lemon” for years without ever having an example of it. Here are four at once, all centred on \(p_{1}\).

Theorem 3.2. \[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S_{492})\cong\;&(S_2\wr S_7)\times S_{11}\times(S_3\wr S_3)\times(S_4\wr S_2)\\&\times(S_2\wr S_2)^2\times S_4\times S_3^{\,3}\times S_2^{\,18}.\end{aligned}\]
Write \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal S_{492})\) and, for an orbit \(O\), \(G^O\le\operatorname{Sym}(O)\) for the induced group. The restriction map \(\rho:G\to\prod_O G^O\) is injective, since \(G\le\operatorname{Sym}(P)\) and \(P\) is the union of the orbits. Sage computes each \(|G^O|\) (table below), and \(\prod_O|G^O|=|G|\); hence \(\rho\) is an isomorphism and \(G\) is the direct product of its orbit groups. For the four large orbits the centre is \(C=\{p_{1}\}\) and every point lies only on its star’s posts, so Lemma 3.1 gives \(S_k\wr S_m\le G^O\); the orders agree, so \(G^O=S_k\wr S_m\). Burnside, factor by factor. Summing \(|\operatorname{Fix}(g)|\) over \(3.3\times10^{30}\) elements is out of the question, but Theorem 3.2 makes Burnside’s lemma easy. For a direct product \(G=\prod_O G^O\) acting on the disjoint union of the \(O\), \(\operatorname{Fix}(g)\) splits over the factors, so \[\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|\operatorname{Fix}(g)|=\sum_{O}\frac1{|G^O|}\sum_{h\in G^O}|\operatorname{Fix}_O(h)|=\sum_O\#\{\text{orbits of }G^O\text{ on }O\}.\] Each \(G^O\) is transitive on its \(O\) (one orbit each, 28 in all), and each of the 73 fixed points is its own orbit: \(28+73=101\), matching Sage. On \(\mathcal S_7\) the same formula gives \(2+8=10\), matching the 120/12 computed by hand above.

The cross-check. \(\{p_{10},p_{11},p_{12}\}\) reappears as one of the three \(S_3\) orbits of \(\mathcal S_{492}\), found with no reference to \(\mathcal S_7\).

Size. The pendant-star factors dominate: \(|S_2\wr S_7|\cdot|S_{11}|\cdot|S_3\wr S_3|\cdot|S_4\wr S_2|\approx3.8\times10^{19}\) before the smaller factors multiply in. Sage finds the orbits from the generators (Schreier–Sims), not by enumerating \(G\).

Reading: 73 accounts are fixed points, the anchors the structure depends on (\(p_{1}\) on all 492 blocks, his foundation on 223, tbfrescue on 179, his film on 24, \(p_{2}\) on 8). The large orbits are pendant stars: posts made of \(p_{1}\) and one-off guests, whose guests are interchangeable both within a post (twins) and across posts (the top group of the wreath product).

5Scope

The structure is only as complete as the captures: \(p_{1}\)’s grid back to September 11, 2025; some accounts capped; captions truncated (which affects only the excluded mention-only posts). “Same orbit” means identical position in this incidence structure, not common control.
